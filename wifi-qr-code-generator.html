Why a static WiFi QR never expires

Many online “WiFi QR” tools create a dynamic code: the printed square points at a short URL on their servers, and that URL later shows your WiFi details—or fails when the trial ends.

A static WiFi QR is different. The SSID, security type, and password are encoded inside the image. After you print or save it:

  • No vendor account is required to keep it working.
  • No subscription renewal can turn it off.
  • There is no short link that can expire or change owners.

That is why a static WiFi QR is a good fit for guest cards, Airbnb check-in packets, café tables, and office lobbies—places where you want the code to keep working for years.

Keep it safe

A WiFi QR is convenient—and it is also a compact copy of your password. Treat it like a written password taped under the router.

Prefer a guest network. Most routers let you create a separate SSID for visitors. Put that network in the QR. Your main devices stay on the primary network; guests get internet without seeing printers, NAS drives, or cameras.

Do not post the QR as a public photo. Uploading a clear photo of your WiFi QR to social media, review sites, or product listings can give strangers your password. Print for on-site use; share digitally only with people you trust.

Remember: the password is in the code. Anyone who can scan it (or decode the image) can read the credentials. If the QR will sit in a semi-public space, guest WiFi with a limited password is safer than your primary home password.

Rotate when needed. If a password leaks or a guest relationship ends, change the guest WiFi password on the router and print a new QR. Static codes cannot be “updated” remotely—you reprint instead. That tradeoff is intentional: no remote control also means no remote kill switch.

How to print and test on iPhone and Android

Print tips

  • Use SVG when possible for sharp edges at any size.
  • Keep quiet zone (white margin) around the code—do not crop tightly.
  • Avoid glossy glare under bright lights; matte card stock scans more reliably.
  • Test one print before ordering a large batch of table tents or stickers.

iPhone

  1. Open the Camera app (Control Center camera works too).
  2. Point at the QR until the WiFi banner appears.
  3. Tap to join and confirm when prompted.
  4. On some iOS versions you may need Settings → Wi-Fi and the scan option if Camera does not offer the join banner.

Android

  1. Open Settings → Network & internet → Internet (wording varies by manufacturer).
  2. Tap the QR / scan icon near Wi-Fi, or use Google Lens / Camera if your device supports it.
  3. Confirm the network name and join.

Quick checklist before you laminate

  • SSID spelling matches the router exactly (including spaces).
  • Security type matches (WPA2 vs open).
  • Password typed correctly—one wrong character breaks the scan experience.
  • Tested on at least one iPhone and one Android device.

FAQ

Does a WiFi QR code expire?

A static WiFi QR does not expire: the SSID and password live in the image, so there is no trial clock to run out. Free static generators that work this way keep working the same way. Codes that “stop working” after a free trial were usually dynamic redirects (a short link behind the square). See why QR codes expire.

What if I change my WiFi password?

Generate and print a new QR. Static codes cannot be edited after creation. That is why guest networks with a password you are willing to rotate are often easier to manage.

Is this WiFi QR generator free?

Yes. Create and download PNG or SVG with no signup. Generation is client-side on your device. Start from this page’s generator or the homepage WiFi tab.

Do guests need an app?

Usually no. Current iPhone Camera and most Android Wi-Fi settings can scan WiFi QR codes. Older phones may need a system update or the manufacturer’s Wi-Fi scan tool.

Can I make a QR for an open network (no password)?

Yes. Set security to no password / nopass and leave the password blank. Still prefer a guest SSID if the network is public-facing.

WPA, WPA2, or WEP—which should I pick?

Use the same setting as your router. For almost all modern home routers with a password, choose WPA/WPA2. Use WEP only if the router is still configured for WEP. Use nopass only for open networks.

Is it safe to put the QR on a front-desk card?

Safer if it is a guest network with limited access. Avoid encoding your primary home or office password on anything that strangers can photograph.
