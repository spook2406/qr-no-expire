Your QR code expired after a free trial

The sticker still looks fine. The camera still finds a square. Then the browser shows an expired campaign, an upgrade wall, or a dead short link. People say “my QR expired after the free trial.” What usually expired is not the barcode format—it is the dynamic redirect (or trial account) behind it.

This guide covers what that pattern means, quick checks before you reprint, what you cannot fix from outside, and how to replace the mark with a static QR you control so a trial clock cannot turn the next print run off.

What “expired after free trial” usually means

Most free-trial QR products encode a vendor-owned short URL in the printed image. While the trial (or paid plan) is active, their servers redirect scanners to your real landing page and may count scans.

When the trial ends, common outcomes include:

  • The short URL or campaign stops resolving.
  • The page asks you to upgrade or log in.
  • The dashboard marks the code inactive.

From a guest’s perspective the QR “expired.” Technically the modules still encode yesterday’s short link; the contract behind that link changed. The pattern can still scan—what failed is the redirect / short link / trial account, not the ink.

Static codes work differently: your final URL, Wi‑Fi string, or text is written into the image. There is no vendor trial attached to the download. Free static generators that embed the payload this way do not share that trial clock. Expiration talk belongs with dynamic free trials and short-link failures. Quick checks before you reprint

Run these before you order a full reprint:

  1. Decode the printed code. Use a second phone or a QR preview app and read the exact URL or payload. Note the host name—do not only trust what the first browser tab shows after redirects.
  2. Own domain vs vendor short domain. If the encoded host is a short domain you do not control, you are almost certainly on a dynamic / trial redirect. If it is your domain (yoursite.com/menu), the barcode is static-style and the problem is usually the page, not a trial kill switch.
  3. Destination 404 vs provider “expired / upgrade” page. A 404 on your site means restore the page or add a redirect. An upgrade wall, “campaign expired,” or login gate on a vendor domain is the trial / plan story.

If every device hits the same vendor expired page at once after a known trial end date, skip deeper print troubleshooting and plan a replacement. For a broader triage tree, see Why did my QR code stop working? (full dead-QR troubleshooting).

What you usually cannot fix from outside

You cannot revive someone else’s redirect without their account and an active plan (subject to their policy). Generating a new code elsewhere does not inherit their short-link ID. A new static QR is a new barcode pointing at a destination you choose.

Physical posters, packaging, and menus that already shipped need replacement—or a sticker overlay—once the old redirect is dead. If you still have dashboard access, renewing may restore redirects without reprinting; confirm in that product’s official Support docs, not hearsay.

Replace it with a static QR you control

  1. Encode the final URL you own (or a Wi‑Fi / text payload). Prefer stable paths you can update in place (yoursite.com/menu, /rsvp, /manual). Avoid nesting another free shortener in front of the real page—that recreates the same cliff.
  2. Generate client-side, then download PNG for screens and SVG for print. Scan-test on iPhone and Android before you send files to the printer.
  3. Soft CTA: use the free static QR generator (no signup) on qrstatic.app—payload in the image, no trial redirect in the middle.

If you later need edit-after-print and vendor analytics, buy a paid dynamic plan on purpose—with clear uptime and exit terms—rather than discovering the cliff after packaging ships.

Prevent the next trial cliff

  • For print permanence, prefer static: the payload lives in the image, so a generator subscription cannot turn the sticker off.
  • Do not put free-trial short links on anything hard to reprint (product boxes, etched signs, wedding suites, laminated menus).
  • If you need edit-after-print, host a URL you control and update the page (or 301) instead of relying on a trial redirect you do not own.
  • If marketing insists on dynamic tracking, start a paid plan before the print deadline—not after.
  • Document the exact encoded string in your brand asset folder so the next reprint matches.

FAQ

Do QR codes expire?

The printed pattern does not carry a built-in calendar. What fails is usually a dynamic redirect or free-trial account, a moved URL (404), changed Wi‑Fi credentials, or damaged print. Static images that embed the payload have no vendor trial timer.

Does a free trial kill static codes?

No. Free static codes that embed your payload in the downloaded image do not depend on a generator subscription to keep scanning. Your website or Wi‑Fi settings can still change—that is separate from a trial kill switch.

Can I keep the same sticker art?

Only if the encoded data still resolves. If the trial redirect is dead, the old art still points at a dead short link. You need a new barcode (new image) for a new destination—or a sticker overlay on the old mark. You cannot “unlock” the old short URL from outside the original vendor account.

Can I extend the old trial code without reprinting?

Only through that vendor’s account and policy. Read their official Support / help center. There is no universal “reactivate any QR” tool.

Should I encode a new shortener in front of my URL?

Adding another free shortener recreates the same risk. Point static codes at the real https URL you intend to keep.

Static replacement vs paid dynamic—how do I choose?

Choose static when the destination can stay stable or you can update the page in place. Choose paid dynamic when you must change destinations without reprinting and you accept ongoing cost. See Static vs dynamic QR codes.

Is “expired QR” ever a phone or print problem?

Sometimes. Dirty lens, glare, low contrast, or damaged print can look like a “dead” code. If the same PNG works on screen but the sticker fails, fix print quality. If every device hits an upgrade wall on a vendor domain, that is the trial / redirect story—see Why did my QR code stop working?.
