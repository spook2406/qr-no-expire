Your QR code expired after a free trial

The sticker still looks perfect. The camera still finds a square. Then the browser shows an expired campaign, an upgrade wall, or a dead short link. People say “my QR expired after the free trial.” What usually expired is not the barcode format—it is the dynamic redirect service behind it.

This article explains that pattern in plain language, what you can (and cannot) fix remotely, and how to replace the code with a static QR you own so a trial clock cannot turn your print run off again.

What “expired after free trial” usually means

Dynamic QR products typically encode a vendor-owned short URL in the printed image. While the trial (or paid plan) is active, their servers redirect scanners to your real landing page and may count scans.

When the trial ends, common outcomes include:

  • The short URL stops redirecting.
  • The page asks you to upgrade or log in.
  • The campaign is marked inactive in their dashboard.

From a guest’s perspective the QR “expired.” Technically the modules still encode yesterday’s short link; the contract behind that link changed.

Free static codes work differently: your URL, WiFi string, or text is written into the image. There is no vendor trial attached to the download. Many free static tools behave this way—not only one site. So it is inaccurate to say “all free QR codes expire.” Expiration talk belongs with dynamic free trials and short-link failures.

For the side-by-side, read static vs dynamic QR codes and QR codes that never expire.

Check the vendor’s own Support docs (do not guess)

Each product defines trial length, grace periods, and what happens to codes at non-payment. Do not rely on hearsay or a blog’s invented quote. Open that vendor’s official Support or help center and read their trial / billing / “what happens when my plan ends” articles for the product you actually used.

While you are there, note:

  • Whether inactive codes can be reactivated by paying.
  • Whether destinations are exportable.
  • Whether the short domain might be retired even for paying users during migrations.

If you still have dashboard access, you may be able to renew and restore redirects without reprinting—sometimes. If you lost access, or the company changed terms, plan on a reprint.

What you cannot do from the outside

You cannot “unlock” a third-party short link by generating a new code elsewhere that magically inherits their ID. A new static QR is a new barcode pointing at a destination you choose. Physical posters, packaging, and menus that already shipped need replacement (or a sticker overlay) once the old redirect is dead.

Exception path: only the original vendor (with a valid account/plan) can revive their short URLs—subject to their policy. Again: confirm in their Support docs.

Replace it with a static QR you own

  1. Choose a durable destination. Prefer a URL on a domain you control (yoursite.com/menu, /rsvp, /manual). For WiFi, use a guest SSID and the WiFi generator.
  2. Generate a static image. Open the free static QR tool—no signup, client-side. Encode URL / Text / WiFi / Email / Phone as needed.
  3. Download PNG or SVG, scan-test on iPhone and Android, then reprint or cover the old marks.
  4. Keep the URL stable. Update page content in place so you are not forced into another print cycle. Restaurants: menu QR that never expires. Events: wedding QR static, no signup. Reviews: Google review QR static.

If you later need editable destinations and vendor analytics, buy a paid dynamic plan on purpose—with clear uptime and exit terms—rather than discovering the cliff after packaging ships.

Soft CTA: the static, no-signup workflow is what qrstatic.app is for once DNS cutover completes; generate on whichever host is live today.

Prevent the next trial cliff

  • Do not put free-trial short links on anything hard to reprint (product boxes, etched signs, wedding suites, laminated menus).
  • Prefer static for evergreen WiFi, support URLs, and review links.
  • If marketing insists on dynamic, start paid before the print deadline—not after.
  • Document the exact encoded string in your brand asset folder.
  • When something fails in the field, triage with why did my QR code stop working.

FAQ

Did the QR image itself expire?

Usually no. The image still encodes data. The redirect service or destination stopped cooperating. Static images have no built-in trial timer.

Are free static QR codes safe from trial expiration?

Free static codes that embed your payload in the downloaded image do not depend on a generator subscription to keep working. Your website or WiFi settings can still change—that is separate from a trial kill switch.

Can I extend the old trial code without reprinting?

Only through that vendor’s account and policy. Read their official Support/help center. There is no universal “reactivate any QR” tool.

Will paying the old vendor restore every printed code?

Often yes if the same short URLs remain on their platform and the domain still resolves. Migrations and shutdowns can still strand codes—confirm with their docs and support before you rely on nostalgia.

Should I encode a new shortener in front of my URL?

Adding another free shortener recreates the same risk. Point static codes at the real https URL you intend to keep.

Static replacement vs paid dynamic—how do I choose?

Choose static when the destination can stay stable or you can update the page in place. Choose paid dynamic when you must change destinations without reprinting and you accept ongoing cost. See static vs dynamic.

Is “expired QR” ever a phone problem?

Sometimes scan UI fails (dirty lens, glare, damaged print). If the same PNG works on screen but the sticker fails, fix print quality. If every device hits an upgrade wall on a vendor domain, that is the trial/redirect story.
