Restaurant menu QR codes that never expire

Guests expect to scan a table tent and open today’s menu. Too many restaurants discover—months later—that the square still scans but the phone lands on an expired trial page or a broken short link. That is not “QR technology dying.” It is usually a dynamic menu SaaS or free redirect that stopped cooperating.

A static restaurant menu QR encodes a URL you control directly in the image. No vendor account keeps it alive. No free-trial clock sits behind the ink. Update the menu page when prices change; reprint only when you change the URL itself.

This guide covers how static menu codes work, when a paid dynamic tool is still worth it, and how to make a permanent menu QR with no signup.

Static menu URL vs dynamic menu SaaS

Static menu QR Dynamic / SaaS menu QR
What is printed Your full menu URL (or PDF link) A vendor short link or app redirect
Who can turn it off Essentially nobody except you (by moving the URL) The vendor—trial end, unpaid invoice, product sunset
Change dishes / prices Edit the same URL’s page or PDF Often edit in a dashboard without reprinting
Monthly fee for the code None for the barcode itself Common after a free trial
Best when You already host a stable menu page and can reprint occasionally You refuse to reprint and need frequent destination swaps and will pay for reliability

Static does not mean the menu is frozen. It means the QR payload is frozen. Point the code at https://yourrestaurant.com/menu (or a short path you promise to keep). Change the HTML or PDF behind that path as often as you like. Guests keep scanning the same table tent.

Dynamic is not evil—it is a paid redirect product. Use it when creative is expensive to reprint (etched signage, large vinyl) and you budget for a serious plan. Do not bake a free dynamic trial into laminated menus you will not touch for two years. When the trial ends, guests see “expired” or a paywall, and you look unprofessional.

How to make a permanent menu QR (no signup)

  1. Pick a stable URL you own. Prefer a path on your own site or a Google Drive / Notion / PDF host you control long-term. Avoid encoding a third-party shortener. If you use a PDF, keep the same share link and update the file in place when possible.
  2. Open a free static generator. Use the homepage static QR tool—client-side, no account. Choose URL, paste the menu address, download PNG (screens) or SVG (crisp print).
  3. Test before the print run. Scan with iPhone Camera and an Android device at table distance, under typical restaurant lighting. Check glare on laminated tents.
  4. Print with quiet zone. Leave a white margin; do not crop the square tightly into a logo collage. Matte finishes scan more reliably than glossy under spotlights.
  5. Document the URL. Write the encoded address on the staff SOP. If someone rebuilds the website, they must keep that path or set a permanent redirect.

Optional soft brand note: the same workflow is the destination of qrstatic.app once the host cutover finishes—same static, no-signup idea.

Need WiFi for guests as well? Pair the menu tent with a separate WiFi QR code on a guest network—not your POS VLAN.

Print and ops tips for restaurants

One URL, many languages. Host /menu with language toggles rather than three different QR codes per table. Fewer codes means fewer reprints when you rebrand.

Seasonal specials. Put daily specials on the same menu page (a banner or section). Do not mint a new dynamic short link every week unless you are paying for that flexibility on purpose.

When you must change the URL. Generate a new static QR and replace table tents in one pass. Static codes cannot be remotely rewritten—that is the tradeoff that also prevents a vendor from killing them. If an old menu QR already failed, check whether the scan opens a third-party domain; if yes, replace with a static code to your site. See why did my QR stop working and QR expired after free trial.

When dynamic still makes sense

Choose a paid, reputable dynamic or menu platform when:

  • You change destinations constantly and refuse any reprint window.
  • You need vendor scan analytics as a primary KPI (static codes have no built-in scan dashboard—you measure visits on your own site).
  • Corporate or franchise rules mandate a specific SaaS.

In those cases, read the contract for trial length, what happens at non-payment, and export options. Free trials are for evaluation—not for the laminated card that sits on every two-top.

FAQ

Do restaurant menu QR codes expire?

Static menu QR codes—where your menu URL is encoded in the image—do not expire because of a generator trial. What expires is often a dynamic free trial or short-link plan behind the square. Keep a URL you control and the printed code stays valid.

Can I update prices without printing new QR codes?

Yes, if the QR points at a stable URL and you edit that page or PDF. The barcode does not store the dish list; it stores the address.

Is a Google Drive or PDF link OK?

Yes for static encoding, if the link remains reachable and you accept that sharing settings matter. Prefer a page on your own domain when you can. Do not wrap the Drive link in yet another free shortener.

Should I use one QR per language?

Usually no. One multilingual menu page is easier to maintain. Multiple static codes are fine if your workflow already separates menus—just track which URL each tent uses.

Static or dynamic for a café that changes the board daily?

If specials live on the same web menu you already update, static is enough. If you need the printed code to point at a brand-new URL every day with zero reprint, budget for paid dynamic—not a free trial.

Will guests need an app to open my menu?

No. Camera apps open ordinary https URLs. Avoid exotic app-only deep links unless you also provide a fallback web page.

How is this different from a “QR menu builder” subscription?

Many builders sell hosting, design, and a dynamic redirect together. You can still host a simple menu page yourself and encode it with a free static generator. You trade dashboard polish for no monthly fee and no trial kill switch on the code.
