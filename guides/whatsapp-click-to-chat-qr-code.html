WhatsApp click-to-chat QR codes for small business

A counter card, table tent, or poster can carry a QR that opens a WhatsApp chat with your business number. The print-safe version is a static click-to-chat code: a public https://wa.me/… link in the image, not a login session.

This is not a WhatsApp Web or Linked Devices QR. Those login codes appear in WhatsApp’s own app or desktop client and pair your phone with a browser or another device. Do not print a login QR on a shop card—that flow is for you, not for customers. A click-to-chat QR does the opposite: a guest scans, their WhatsApp (app or web) opens a conversation with the number you published, and they still confirm send. Scanning does not message you by itself.

What the QR actually encodes

A static click-to-chat code stores an ordinary URL in the image, built from:

  • Your number in international form (country calling code + national number, digits only—for example 15551234567, not a local number alone).
  • Optionally, a prefilled message as a text query on that URL.

The result looks like https://wa.me/15551234567 or the same URL with a percent-encoded message. There is no qrstatic.app short link in the middle, and no generator account that must stay active for the print to keep encoding that URL.

When someone scans, the phone opens WhatsApp (or WhatsApp Web, depending on the device). A chat with your number appears; a prefilled message shows as a draft. The person still taps send—or they edit or cancel. You have not been messaged until they do. Use this for a number you are allowed to publish, on a card people choose to scan. Do not use it to assemble unsolicited blasts.

If the business number changes, the old print still points at the old number—generate a new image and replace the card. WhatsApp can also change how wa.me links open after an app update; retest if reports start to look like “the QR broke.” The image has no vendor trial clock. Failures are usually a typo, a number no longer on WhatsApp, or a dynamic short link placed in front of wa.me.

How to make a print-ready chat QR

  1. Use the international number customers should message. Digits only; include the country calling code. A leading zero on the national part is normally dropped once the country code is set. After cleanup, an E.164-style string is typically 8 to 15 digits.
  2. Add a prefilled message only if it helps. A short line such as “Hi—I scanned the counter QR about catering” can save typing. Some WhatsApp clients truncate very long drafts (over about 500 characters may not arrive intact). It is still a draft until they send.
  3. Generate the static image in the browser. Soft CTA: use the WhatsApp click-to-chat QR generator—no signup; the number and message stay on your device. Download SVG for print and PNG for screens and proofs.
  4. Label the square and proof-scan it. Write “Chat with us on WhatsApp” (or similar) so nobody mistakes it for Wi‑Fi or a menu. Confirm the draft and number on iPhone and Android, then print. Start around 2–2.5 cm on a counter card, or 3–4 cm on a table tent, with a white quiet zone.

If you put a trial shortener in front of wa.me to “track scans,” you are back to a redirect that can die. Prefer the raw wa.me URL unless you are paying for a redirect you trust.

FAQ

Is this a WhatsApp Web or Linked Devices login QR?

No. Login and device-pairing codes are created inside WhatsApp to tie your session to a browser or another device. This guide and the generator encode a public click-to-chat URL (https://wa.me/…). They do not create a session, pair a device, or log anyone in.

Is this official WhatsApp?

No. qrstatic.app is an independent static QR tool and is not affiliated with WhatsApp or Meta. It only formats a wa.me link. Follow WhatsApp’s current terms when you collect and message customers.

Does scanning send a WhatsApp message automatically?

No. The scan opens a chat—and a draft, if you included prefilled text. The person still reviews it and sends, or they cancel. That is why this belongs on a card people choose to scan, not an unsolicited campaign.

Does the WhatsApp QR expire?

The static image does not depend on a generator trial or a redirect this site hosts; it keeps the wa.me URL you created. A number you no longer control, a typo, or a change in how WhatsApp opens those links can still make the chat useless. Retest after major app updates, and reprint if the number changes. If the square opens a third-party “expired / upgrade” page, someone likely encoded a dynamic short link instead of wa.me.

Can I include a prefilled message?

Yes, as a text query on the wa.me URL. Keep it short and specific; some WhatsApp clients truncate very long drafts. The customer can still edit it before sending.

What phone number format should I use?

Use the country calling code plus the national number, digits only (for example 44 then the UK number without a leading zero). A local-only number without a country code is a common reason the chat opens wrong. If a generator rejects the value, the cleaned digit string is often too short, too long, or missing the country code.

Can I put a WhatsApp QR on the same table tent as menu, Wi‑Fi, and review codes?

Yes, if each square is clearly labeled and large enough to scan. Treat chat as an optional extra—not a replacement for guest Wi‑Fi or a review link. See the café table tent guide.
