QR code guides

A curated set of practical guides for static, no-signup QR codes. Each one matches a generator on qrstatic.app.

These pages explain a specific decision or a specific failure, then link to the tool that builds the payload in your browser. Start with the question you actually have. The cards at the bottom are the full index.

If you are choosing static or dynamic

A static code stores the payload in the image. A dynamic code stores a short link that a vendor must keep resolving. The difference decides whether a table tent can die when a trial ends.

  • Static vs dynamic QR codes — what is printed, whether you can edit it later, and when a redirect is worth paying for.
  • Is my QR static or dynamic? — read the scanned text. Your own URL or a WIFI: string is static; a short domain you do not control is a redirect.
  • QR codes that never expire — why “expired” usually means the lookup died, not that the ink wore out.
  • Your QR code expired after a free trial — what a trial cliff is, and how to replace that print with a static file.
  • Free forever QR code — what to watch for — marketing that is free until the redirect is not.
  • Static QR codes that never expire — how a permanent payload is built, and when it is the wrong tool.
  • Why did my QR code stop working? — dead redirects, moved URLs, Wi‑Fi changes, and damaged print.

If you are printing for a cafe, shop, or event

These are the jobs the generators are aimed at: a guest network, a review card, a menu URL you host, a tent that holds more than one code. None of them edit themselves after they are on the table.

  • Guest Wi‑Fi QR for a cafe or restaurant — put a static WIFI: code on a table tent, test iPhone and Android, and plan the reprint before the password rotates.
  • Wi‑Fi QR not working after a password change — the old sticker still has the old password. Generate a new image and replace the card. There is no remote update.
  • Free Google review QR with no monthly fee — encode your own Place review link so a subscription short link is not sitting between the counter card and Google.
  • Static Google review QR codes — the same idea, with the compliance limits: not affiliated with Google, and no gated or incentivized reviews.
  • Cafe table tent: menu, Wi‑Fi, and reviews — three separate static codes on one tent, with sizes and why they should not be merged into one redirect.
  • Restaurant menu QR that never expires — point the square at a menu page you control, and change the page rather than renting a QR subscription.
  • QR print size by scan distance — practical sizes for cards, tents, and posters, plus quiet zone, contrast, and SVG versus PNG.
  • Wedding QR codes without signup — RSVP, registry, map, and album links as static files, so a trial does not end before the date.

Matching tools: the Wi‑Fi generator, the Google review generator, and the location generator (a Maps URL, or a geo: coordinate pair when you have tested the phones). The homepage generator covers a plain URL, which is what a menu page needs.
