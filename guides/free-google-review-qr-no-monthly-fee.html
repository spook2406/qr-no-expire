Free Google review QR code for small business (no monthly fee)

You want more Google reviews. A printed QR at the counter is still one of the simplest prompts—if the code keeps working after you print it. Some “review QR” tools wrap your link in a short URL they own. When the free trial ends, the sticker still scans, but guests land on an error page instead of your Google profile.

This guide shows a static approach: encode the official Google review (or Maps place) link in an image you keep. No monthly fee for the QR itself. Generate it with the free browser tool: Google review QR code generator.

The free‑trial failure mode (and how static avoids it)

Dynamic review QRs often work like this: printed square → vendor short link → Google review form. The vendor can edit the destination later. That flexibility is useful until billing lapses, the project is deleted, or the trial clock hits zero. Then every card on every table fails at once.

A static QR skips the middle hop. The payload is your Google URL. There is no vendor dashboard to expire. Tradeoff: if Google ever changes that exact URL shape, you reprint—rare compared with trial cliffs. Background: QR code expired after free trial and static vs dynamic QR codes.

What you need

  1. A Google Business Profile for the location you want reviews on.
  2. The official review link or Maps place URL Google gives you (copy from the profile tools or Maps share sheet).
  3. A place to print: counter card, table‑tent panel, sticker near pickup, or insert with takeaway.

You do not need a review‑SaaS account for the QR image. You still follow Google’s rules for soliciting reviews (no fake reviews, no forbidden incentives).

Step‑by‑step: free static Google review QR

  1. Copy your Google review or place URL exactly—no tracking wrappers you do not control.
  2. Open the Google review QR generator on qrstatic.app.
  3. Paste the URL, generate, and scan with a phone signed into a Google account that can leave reviews.
  4. Confirm the phone opens the review flow or place page you expect.
  5. Download PNG for quick prints and SVG as the master file. Store the SVG with the URL text in a notes file.

Placement that actually gets taps

  • At payment or pickup — guests already have phones out.
  • Labeled clearly — “Leave a Google review” beats an unlabeled square next to Wi‑Fi.
  • One job per code — do not combine review + Wi‑Fi into a single mystery payload.
  • Readable size — follow print size and scan distance so the camera locks quickly.

If the square never detects, check damage and contrast with why did my QR code stop working before blaming Google.

Pair with Wi‑Fi and menu codes

Table tents often carry menu + Wi‑Fi + review. Keep three separate codes with short labels so a Wi‑Fi password rotation does not force a full reprint of your review panel. Layout ideas: cafe table tent QR codes. Guest Wi‑Fi how‑to: guest Wi‑Fi QR for cafes. Menu permanence: restaurant menu QR.

When dynamic still makes sense

Choose a paid redirect product only if you must change destinations without reprinting (seasonal landing pages, A/B tests, or multi‑location packs you reassign often)—and you budget for the subscription. Watch trial dates. For most single‑location shops, a static Google URL is enough.

Checklist before you print a batch

  • Scan the code on iOS and Android if you can.
  • Confirm the landing page is the correct location, not a nearby duplicate listing.
  • Print one proof on the real stock (tent card or sticker) under shop lighting.
  • Train staff to point at the labeled square instead of opening Maps themselves.
  • Keep the SVG and the raw Google URL in the same folder so a reprint takes minutes.

If you already printed dynamic trial stickers, replace them on a schedule rather than waiting for angry “it won’t open” feedback. Guests rarely tell you the short link died—they just leave.

What this is not

A free static review QR does not buy you reviews, does not auto‑post to Google, and does not bypass Google’s policies. It also will not fix a wrong place URL you pasted on day one. Re‑test after you move locations or claim a different profile.
